# Design Rationale — Math Enthusiast (undergraduate seminar)

**Decisions confirmed:** undergrad seminar · taught with deliverables · flagship-per-section first  
**Date:** 2026-08-03  
**Skill:** course-designer (outcomes → evidence → schedule)

---

## Why this shape?

Math Enthusiast began as a **topic map** of modern mathematics. For a **taught seminar**, the map stays, but the spine becomes **six measurable capabilities** (LO1–LO6), not “cover all posts.”

Backward design (Wiggins & McTighe): we do **not** require students to read every short stub. We require them to **do** the outcomes, using **one deep flagship per section** as the shared intellectual experience.

---

## Outcomes (why these six)

| LO | Role in the course |
|----|--------------------|
| LO1 Explain a hard problem | Core identity of Sections 1–2 |
| LO2 Measure vs dimension | Prevents the most common Kakeya misconception; analytic literacy |
| LO3 Proof-idea narrative | Section 5 culture: idea ≠ full technical writeup |
| LO4 Field → application mechanism | Section 3–6 without buzzword-only answers |
| LO5 Exploration portfolio | Section 7 as *create*-level evidence |
| LO6 Critique popular math | Media literacy for Fields/open-problem hype |

Bloom mix: understand / analyze / apply / create / evaluate — not all “appreciate.”

---

## Assessment structure (why these weights)

| Assessment | Weight | Why |
|------------|--------|-----|
| A1 Participation | 15% | Seminar lives or dies on discussion (Tier P) |
| A2 Reading responses | 15% | Spaced retrieval; low-stakes before papers (best 5 of 6) |
| A3 Open-problem brief | 15% | Direct LO1 evidence mid-term |
| A4 Proof-idea narrative | 15% | Direct LO3 evidence |
| A5 Exploration portfolio | 25% | Highest *create* demand; staged process resists pure AI dump |
| A6 Synthesis essay | 15% | Capstone map + critique (LO4, LO6) |

No single item >40%. First major paper (A3) after three response cycles.

AI policy: live discussion = Tier P; process-heavy portfolio = redesigned for authenticity; synthesis allows Tier O with process note.

## Schedule arc (15 weeks)

1. **Orient** (W1)  
2. **Problems & breakthroughs** (W2–W5) including existing Wang flagship  
3. **Applications & beauty** (W6–W7)  
4. **Proof culture** (W8–W9)  
5. **Frontiers** (W10)  
6. **Exploration studio** (W11–W13)  
7. **Synthesis** (W14–W15)  

Interleaving: LO1 returns in A6; LO2 appears in Wang week and exploration; LO6 appears early and late.

## Flagship-per-section (content strategy)

| Section | Flagship | Status |
|---------|----------|--------|
| 01 Great Problems | RH or P vs NP deep essay | **todo** |
| 02 Fields | Wang / Kakeya | **done** |
| 03 World-changing | Crypto or LA→ML | **todo** |
| 04 Beautiful | Infinity or fractals | **todo** |
| 05 Proofs | Euclid+Cantor or FLT idea | **todo** |
| 06 Future | Math of AI or quantum info | **todo** |
| 07 Explorations | One scaffolded studio guide | **todo** |

Thin map posts remain as optional browsing; they are not weekly required reading unless assigned as comparison fodder.

---

## What we deliberately did *not* do

- Invent institutional course code, term dates, or integrity URLs.  
- Require covering all ~70 topic posts.  
- Sit-down timed final (misaligned with idea-based outcomes).  
- Grade pure chat-produced essays without process evidence.
