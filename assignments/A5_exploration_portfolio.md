## Purpose

Practice **experimental mathematics**: question → experiment → honest report. Default studio: **Collatz / iteration** ([studio guide](../contents/en/chapter07/_posts/21-01-01-07_09_Explore_Iteration.md)). Alternate Section 7 topics only with instructor approval—same milestones.

## Milestone 1 — Proposal (W11)

**Length:** 1–2 pages.

Include:

1. Question (falsifiable / investigable—not “prove Collatz”)  
2. Definitions (e.g. total stopping time)  
3. Methods & tools  
4. Success criteria (plots, ranges, number of experiments)  
5. Risks (overflow, bias)  
6. AI plan (if any)

**Pass gate.** Proposal must be approved or marked “revise once” before the report is graded in full. Late proposal eats free late days.

## Milestone 2 — Process log (W13)

Dated entries with: intent · action · result · interpretation · next step.  
**Failed attempts required as evidence of process** (at least one dead end discussed).

## Milestone 3 — Report (W15)

**Length:** about 6–10 pages equivalent (figures count).

Outline:

1. Introduction & non-goals (what you are not proving)  
2. Definitions & methods  
3. Experiments (≥2) with results  
4. Conjectures formed / discarded  
5. Limitations  
6. Course connections (open problems, verification vs proof)  
7. References + AI disclosure  

## Rubric

| Criterion | Weight (of A5) | Excellent | Needs work |
|-----------|---------------:|-----------|------------|
| **Question & design (LO5)** | 25% | Clear, testable, methods fit | Vague fishing expedition |
| **Log quality** | 25% | Dated, honest, iterative | Empty or reverse-engineered |
| **Experiments & correctness** | 25% | ≥2 solid experiments; small cases correct | Broken defs / no real trials |
| **Interpretation & limits** | 15% | Separates observation vs conjecture | Overclaims proof |
| **Communication** | 10% | Clear figures/writing | Unreadable |

Milestone weights inside A5 (suggested): proposal 20% · log 30% · report 50% of the 25% (i.e. 5% / 7.5% / 12.5% of course grade)—instructor may equal-weight if preferred.

## What not to do

- Fabricate log entries after the fact.
- Treat large computational checks as a proof of Collatz.
- Submit only code with no scientific narrative.
