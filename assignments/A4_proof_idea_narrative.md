# A4 — Proof-idea narrative

| | |
|--|--|
| **Weight** | 15% |
| **Due** | Week 9 |
| **Length** | 1200–1500 words |
| **Outcomes** | LO3 |
| **AI tier** | **D** — tools with disclosure + process note |

## Purpose

Train reconstruction of a **proof idea**: logical skeleton and key insight a classmate can follow—not a fully formal writeup and not a biography of the author.

## Task

Choose **one** proof/breakthrough idea treated in the course, for example:

- Euclid: infinitely many primes  
- Cantor: uncountability / diagonal argument  
- Irrationality of \(\sqrt{2}\)  
- Königsberg / Eulerian paths (model + degree condition)  
- Idea-level sketch of a modern result **only if** you stick to idea-not-details (e.g. FLT modularity *story*, not a fake proof)

**Required structure (use headings)**

1. **Theorem in plain language**  
2. **Setup / definitions** (minimal)  
3. **Assumption or plan** (contradiction, construction, …)  
4. **Key construction** (the \(N=P+1\); the diagonal digit rule; …)  
5. **Why the witness works**  
6. **What is not claimed** (hygiene: composite \(N\); non-unique decimals; …)  
7. **Why the idea matters** (one short paragraph)  
8. **AI process note** if tools used  

Default preparation: flagship [Euclid and Cantor: Two Ideas of Proof](../contents/en/chapter05/_posts/21-01-01-05_02_Euclid_Infinite_Primes.md).

## Rubric

| Criterion | Excellent | Satisfactory | Needs work |
|-----------|-----------|--------------|------------|
| **Skeleton (LO3)** | Complete logical path; classmate could retell | Small gaps | Missing steps or wrong logic |
| **Idea vs detail** | Clear separation; hygiene named | Somewhat mixed | Pretends full formal proof or only handwaves |
| **Construction** | Correct key construction | Minor error | Wrong construction |
| **Clarity** | Clean narrative | Readable | Confusing |
| **Integrity** | Disclosure + checks | Thin disclosure | Undisclosed AI / invented steps |

## Peer review (W9)

Bring a draft. Reviewer checklist: Can I restate the construction? Is one hygiene caveat present? Any false claim?

## What not to do

- Submit only a historical essay.
- Copy a textbook proof without the idea-level explanation in your voice.
- For Cantor: ignore the list-escape idea and only talk about “infinity is big.”
