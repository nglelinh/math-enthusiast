# A3 — Open-problem brief

| | |
|--|--|
| **Weight** | 15% |
| **Due** | Week 5 |
| **Length** | 1200–1500 words |
| **Outcomes** | LO1, LO2 (as applicable) |
| **AI tier** | **D** — tools with disclosure + short process note |

## Purpose

Show you can **explain a major open (or recently resolved) problem** the way a mathematician would to a peer: precise statement, hardness, surrounding mathematics—not a solution and not pure hype.

## Task

Write a brief on **one** problem. Strong default choices from this course:

- Riemann Hypothesis (flagship available)
- P vs NP
- Twin primes / bounded gaps story
- Kakeya dimension problem (use Wang flagship carefully: 3D resolved; higher \(n\) open)
- BSD, Navier–Stokes, Collatz (state status carefully)

**Required sections**

1. **Statement.** Precise enough for a classmate who read the relevant flagship/map page.
2. **Why it matters.** One paragraph: what hinges on it (error terms, crypto culture, geometry, …).
3. **Why it is hard.** At least two distinct reasons (not “because it’s old”).
4. **Surrounding mathematics.** Name ≥1 tool/theory grown around it (e.g. Euler product & explicit formulas; sieves; δ-tubes; complexity classes).
5. **Status & evidence.** What is known (partial results, numerical checks) vs open. If recently resolved, state who/when and what remains open nearby.
6. **Size/regularity note (LO2 when natural).** If relevant (Kakeya, primes, infinity-adjacent), compare two notions of size or regularity and correct one common confusion.
7. **Sources.** ≥3 sources (flagship + ≥1 other). Prefer primary surveys/Clay pages/arXiv over random blogs.
8. **AI process note** (if tools used): what you asked, what you verified, what you rewrote.

## Rubric

| Criterion | Excellent | Satisfactory | Needs work |
|-----------|-----------|--------------|------------|
| **Statement (LO1)** | Precise; no slogan-only; status correct | Mostly precise; minor slips | Vague or wrong status |
| **Hardness & context** | ≥2 real reasons; surrounding math named correctly | Thin but not wrong | Missing or mythical |
| **Evidence literacy** | Clear verification vs proof | Mentions evidence loosely | Treats numerics as proof |
| **LO2 (if applicable)** | Clean size-notion contrast | Partial | Reinforces misconception |
| **Writing & sources** | Clear structure; good sources | Usable | Chaotic / unsourced |
| **Integrity** | Disclosure + verification | Disclosure thin | Undisclosed AI dump |

## What not to do

- Claim you solved the problem.
- Confuse popular metaphors with definitions.
- For Kakeya: claim “all dimensions solved” or ignore joint Wang–Zahl credit when discussing 3D.
