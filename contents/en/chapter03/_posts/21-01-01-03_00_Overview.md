Open your phone. Encrypt a message. Compress a photo. Ask a model to classify an image. Route a package. Predict a storm.

Somewhere under each of those acts sits a mathematical idea that once looked abstract—or even idle. Calculus was not invented for rockets, yet rockets speak calculus. Number theory was once the purest pure mathematics; today it holds the keys to online trust. Linear algebra was about lines and planes long before it became the mother tongue of machine learning.

This chapter is about **those migrations**: from blackboard to infrastructure.

We will not pretend every theorem was written for industry. Often the opposite is true: someone chased beauty or necessity inside mathematics, and the world later discovered it needed exactly that shape of thought. The stories here track **mechanisms**, not slogans—how modular arithmetic becomes a public-key handshake, how a Fourier transform becomes a JPEG, how a gradient becomes a training step.

If Chapter 1 asks what remains unsolved, this chapter asks a different question:

**What has already escaped the university and rewired daily life—and what precise idea made the escape possible?**
