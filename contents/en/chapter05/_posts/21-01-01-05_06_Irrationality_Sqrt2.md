More than two and a half millennia ago, the Pythagoreans of ancient Greece held a near-religious conviction: *"All is number"*—meaning every length, musical harmony, and cosmic proportion could be captured by a **ratio of two whole numbers**. Yet hiding inside the simplest geometric figure imaginable—a square of side $$1$$—lurked a rebel: a diagonal of length $$\sqrt{2}$$ that refused to be measured by any common ruler.

Legend says that when Hippasus of Metapontum exposed this "unspeakable" (*alogon*) secret, it triggered the first foundational crisis in mathematics. Even today, the proof remains one of the finest first arguments in all of mathematics: in just a handful of lines, pure **even-and-odd parity** splits the assumption of rationality wide open.

**Lesson roadmap:** Background Knowledge Map (number-system tower, $$p$$-adic valuation, infinite descent) → The common-ruler paradox and Pell’s near-miss fractions → The classical lowest-terms proof → Deep-Dive Explanation (Read Slowly): hand-checking three perspectives ($$2$$-adic valuation $$v_2$$, geometric two-squares descent, and why $$\sqrt{4}$$ escapes) → Generalizing to $$\sqrt{p}$$ and extending number systems.

## Background Knowledge Map: Number Systems, $$p$$-adic Valuation, and Infinite Descent

To see why such a brief proof sits beside Euclid and Gödel, place it on three pillars of **Number Theory** and **Algebra**:

### 1. The Tower of Number Systems ($$\mathbb{N} \subset \mathbb{Z} \subset \mathbb{Q} \subset \mathbb{R} \subset \mathbb{C}$$)
Whenever a natural operation hits a wall, mathematics expands the universe of numbers: counting $$\mathbb{N}$$ cannot always subtract (inventing $$\mathbb{Z}$$); integers cannot always divide (inventing $$\mathbb{Q}$$); rationals leave "gaps" like the diagonal $$\sqrt{2}$$ (inventing $$\mathbb{R}$$); and $$x^2 + 1 = 0$$ invents $$\mathbb{C}$$. Inside the irrationals, $$\sqrt{2}$$ is an **algebraic number** (root of $$x^2 - 2 = 0$$), unlike **transcendental numbers** such as $$\pi$$ and $$e$$ (roots of no integer polynomial at all).

### 2. Divisibility and $$p$$-adic Valuation ($$v_p$$)
Modern number theory upgrades "even vs. odd" to counting the **highest power of a prime $$p$$** dividing an integer $$n$$—its **$$p$$-adic valuation** $$v_p(n)$$. For instance, $$v_2(12) = v_2(2^2 \cdot 3) = 2$$ and $$v_2(24) = v_2(2^3 \cdot 3) = 3$$. Because $$v_p(ab) = v_p(a) + v_p(b)$$, the valuation of any square $$v_p(a^2) = 2v_p(a)$$ is **always even**—a one-line weapon that destroys $$p^2 = 2q^2$$ on sight.

### 3. Well-Ordering and Infinite Descent
The positive integers $$\mathbb{N}^*$$ have a hard floor at $$1$$: **no strictly decreasing sequence of positive integers can go on forever** ($$a_1 > a_2 > a_3 > \cdots > 0$$ is impossible). **Infinite descent** (later perfected by Fermat for $$a^4 + b^4 = c^4$$) turns that floor into a proof engine: if any hypothetical solution forces a strictly smaller positive integer solution, no solution can exist.

## 1. From the "Finer Ruler" Paradox to Incommensurability

In engineering and daily life, it feels obvious that if we keep subdividing a ruler—millimeters, micrometers, nanometers—some ultra-fine tick mark $$1/q$$ must eventually measure *both* the side and diagonal of a unit square evenly. Test **Pell’s sequence** of best rational approximations generated by $$p_{n+1} = p_n + 2q_n,\ q_{n+1} = p_n + q_n$$ (starting from $$p_1 = 1, q_1 = 1$$):

| Step $$n$$ | Fraction $$p_n/q_n$$ | Decimal value | $$p_n^2$$ | $$2q_n^2$$ | Gap $$p_n^2 - 2q_n^2$$ |
|------------|----------------------|---------------|-----------|------------|------------------------|
| $$1$$ | $$1/1$$ | $$1.00000$$ | $$1$$ | $$2$$ | $$-1$$ |
| $$2$$ | $$3/2$$ | $$1.50000$$ | $$9$$ | $$8$$ | $$+1$$ |
| $$3$$ | $$7/5$$ | $$1.40000$$ | $$49$$ | $$50$$ | $$-1$$ |
| $$4$$ | $$17/12$$ | $$1.41667$$ | $$289$$ | $$288$$ | $$+1$$ |
| $$5$$ | $$41/29$$ | $$1.41379$$ | $$1681$$ | $$1682$$ | $$-1$$ |
| $$6$$ | $$99/70$$ | $$1.41429$$ | $$9801$$ | $$9800$$ | $$+1$$ |

We can get arbitrarily close to $$\sqrt{2} \approx 1.41421356\ldots$$, yet the integer equation $$p^2 = 2q^2$$ **always misses by at least $$1$$**! Two lengths are **commensurable** if a common unit measures both an integer number of times; the theorem below proves the side and diagonal of a square are forever incommensurable.

## 2. Theorem and setup

<div class="textbook-theorem" markdown="1">
**Theorem.** $$\sqrt{2}$$ is irrational: there are no integers $$p,q$$ with $$q\neq 0$$ such that $$\sqrt{2}=p/q$$.
</div>

Equivalently: the equation

$$
p^2 = 2q^2
$$

has no solutions in integers with $$q\neq 0$$.

---

## 3. The classical proof (idea with all essential steps)

Assume, for contradiction, that $$\sqrt{2}=p/q$$ where $$p,q$$ are integers, $$q>0$$, and the fraction is in **lowest terms**: $$\gcd(p,q)=1$$.

Then $$p^2 = 2q^2$$. The right-hand side is even, so $$p^2$$ is even. If $$p$$ were odd ($$p = 2m+1$$), then $$p^2 = 4m^2 + 4m + 1$$ would be odd; hence $$p$$ must be even. Write $$p = 2k$$ and substitute:

$$
(2k)^2 = 2q^2 \implies 4k^2 = 2q^2 \implies q^2 = 2k^2.
$$

Thus $$q^2$$ is even, so $$q$$ is even. Now $$2$$ divides both $$p$$ and $$q$$, contradicting $$\gcd(p,q)=1$$. Therefore $$\sqrt{2}$$ is irrational.

## 4. Deep-Dive Explanation (Read Slowly): Three Hand-Verifiable Perspectives

To see past the three lines of algebra and feel the deeper structure, verify three concrete calculations by hand:

### Perspective 1: Hand-Check the $$2$$-adic Valuation $$v_2$$
Pick any two positive integers—say $$p = 12 = 2^2 \cdot 3$$ and $$q = 10 = 2^1 \cdot 5$$:
- Left side: $$p^2 = 144 = 2^4 \cdot 9 \implies v_2(p^2) = 4$$ (**even**).
- Right side: $$2q^2 = 200 = 2^3 \cdot 25 \implies v_2(2q^2) = 1 + 2(1) = 3$$ (**odd**).

In general, for *every* positive integer pair $$(p, q)$$, $$v_2(p^2) = 2v_2(p)$$ is **even** while $$v_2(2q^2) = 1 + 2v_2(q)$$ is **odd**. No integer can have an exponent of $$2$$ that is simultaneously even and odd!

### Perspective 2: Verify the Geometric Two-Squares Descent (Tennenbaum / Apostol)
Suppose $$(a, b)$$ is the *smallest* positive integer pair satisfying $$a^2 = 2b^2$$ (one $$a \times a$$ square has the exact area of two $$b \times b$$ squares). Since $$1 < \sqrt{2} < 2$$, we have $$b < a < 2b$$.
- Place two $$b \times b$$ squares into opposite corners of the $$a \times a$$ square.
- They **overlap** in a central square of side $$a' = 2b - a > 0$$.
- They leave **two uncovered corner squares**, each of side $$b' = a - b > 0$$.
- Because the two $$b \times b$$ squares have the same total area as the $$a \times a$$ square, the overlapped area **must equal** the uncovered area:
  $$
  (2b - a)^2 \;=\; 2(a - b)^2.
  $$
- **Verify the algebra by hand:**
  - Left side: $$(2b - a)^2 = 4b^2 - 4ab + a^2$$.
  - Right side: $$2(a - b)^2 = 2a^2 - 4ab + 2b^2$$.
  - Cancel $$-4ab$$ from both sides: $$4b^2 + a^2 = 2a^2 + 2b^2 \iff 2b^2 = a^2$$ (exact identity!).
- Yet $$a' = 2b - a < a$$ and $$b' = a - b < b$$ form a **strictly smaller** positive integer solution—contradicting minimality!

### Perspective 3: Sanity Check — Why Does the Proof Break for $$\sqrt{4}$$?
Try running the same argument on $$p^2 = 4q^2$$:
- The right side is divisible by $$4 \implies 4 \mid p^2$.
- But "$$4 \mid p^2 \Rightarrow 4 \mid p$$" is **FALSE!** (For $$p = 2, q = 1$$, $$p^2 = 4$$ is divisible by $$4$$ even though $$p = 2$$ is not.)
- In valuation language: $$v_2(4q^2) = 2 + 2v_2(q)$$ is **even**, matching $$v_2(p^2) = 2v_2(p)$$—so $$\sqrt{4} = 2$$ safely survives!

## 5. Infinite descent formulation

One can rephrase without an explicit gcd. Suppose $$p^2=2q^2$$ with positive integers $$p,q$$. Then $$p$$ is even, $$p=2k$$, and $$q^2=2k^2$$, so $$q$$ is even, $$q=2\ell$$, and $$k^2 = 2\ell^2$$ with strictly smaller positive integers $$k < p$$. Repeating forever yields an infinite decreasing sequence of positive integers $$p > k > \cdots > 0$$, which is impossible.

---

## 6. What the idea *is* and what it is *not*

**What the idea *is*:** assume rationality in normalized (lowest-terms) form, transfer divisibility from $$p^2$$ to $$p$$ via prime divisibility (or $$v_2$$ parity), and contradict minimality.

**What the idea is *not*:**
- It is not “the decimal of $$\sqrt{2}$$ never terminates, therefore irrational.” Many rationals have non-terminating decimals ($$1/3=0.333\ldots$$). Non-terminating *and non-repeating* is equivalent to irrationality, but proving non-repetition is not easier than parity.  
- It is not a claim about approximations: $$\sqrt{2}$$ *is* a limit of rationals; irrationality denies equality with a ratio, not approximability.  
- It does not prove transcendence ($$\sqrt{2}$$ is algebraic).

---

## 7. Extending the pattern: $$\sqrt{3}$$, $$\sqrt{p}$$

For $$\sqrt{3}$$, assume $$p^2=3q^2$$ in lowest terms. Then $$3\mid p^2$$ implies $$3\mid p$$ (because 3 is prime), write $$p=3k$$, obtain $$q^2=3k^2$$, hence $$3\mid q$$, contradiction. The upgraded lemma is **Euclid’s lemma**: *if a prime divides a product (or a square), it divides a factor.* For any prime $$p$$, $$\sqrt{p}$$ is irrational; for any non-square integer $$n$$, $$\sqrt{n}$$ is irrational because some prime $$p$$ divides $$n$$ to an odd power, making $$v_p(n q^2)$$ odd.

---

## 8. Why it matters

- **Prototype of number-theoretic rigor.** Assumptions about integers yield contradictions via divisibility.  
- **Gateway to algebra.** Polynomial roots, algebraic integers, and field extensions all sit downstream of “not rational.”  
- **Proof culture.** Normalization (lowest terms), parity lemmas, and descent are reusable tools.  
- **Historical consciousness.** Incommensurability forced Greek mathematics beyond pure rational measure.

Terence Tao, in a [Big Think conversation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OOMx2BHHWtE) for *Six Math Essentials*, places that shock inside a longer **number-system extension** story. Counting suggests addition and subtraction; subtraction invents the negatives and zero so that $$(A-B)+B=A$$ still holds when $$B>A$$. Division invents fractions. Then lengths appear that sit between all the fractions: $$\sqrt{2}$$ cannot be a ratio, and the Latin *irrational* records how unreasonable that felt. Later, square roots that refuse to stay real produce the complex numbers, which become a natural language for electromagnetism and quantum mechanics. The moral is not “decimals go on forever.” It is that new numbers are often invented so the old algebraic laws close—and then turn out to describe the world. Hub: [Six Math Essentials]({{ site.baseurl }}/contents/en/chapter04/04_01_Six_Math_Essentials/).

## Common confusions

1. **“Non-terminating decimal ⇒ irrational.”** — False; $$1/3$$ terminates only in the sense of repeating.  
2. **“Lowest terms is optional decoration.”** — Without minimality or descent, “both even” is not yet a contradiction.  
3. **“The proof shows $$\sqrt{2}$$ cannot be approximated by rationals.”** — False; rationals approximate it arbitrarily well.  
4. **“$$p^2$$ even implies $$p$$ even” is special magic for 2.”** — For prime moduli the analogue is Euclid’s lemma.  
5. **“Idea of proof means skip the odd-square lemma.”** — That lemma *is* the idea’s hinge; name it.

### Core statement / slogan

$$\sqrt{2}$$ is irrational: if $$a/b$$ in lowest terms with $$a^2=2b^2$$, then $$a$$ and $$b$$ are both even — contradiction.

### Definitions to freeze

- **Rational.** $$x=a/b$$ with $$a,b\in\mathbb{Z}$$, $$b\neq 0$$.
- **Infinite descent (idea).** From a positive integer solution produce a strictly smaller one — impossible.

### Hygiene (from confusions log)

- Thinking the proof only works for √2 and not primes $$p\equiv 3\pmod 4$$ etc. without adapting the parity argument.
- Confusing irrationality with transcendence.
