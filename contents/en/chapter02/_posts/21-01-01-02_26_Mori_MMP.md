**Shigefumi Mori** received the **Fields Medal 1990** for launching and, in dimension three, completing the core of what Heisuke Hironaka’s ICM text calls **Mori’s Program**: a constructive classification scheme for algebraic threefolds up to birational equivalence. Hironaka wrote that the most profound development of the preceding decade in algebraic geometry was this Minimal Model Program for varieties of dimension three, and that Mori “finished up the program by himself overcoming the last difficulty.” The last difficulty was the **existence of three-fold flips**. The program did **not**, in 1990 or later, finish classification in all dimensions.

This essay is for learners who have seen curves and surfaces and want the architecture of higher-dimensional birational geometry at the moment it became a machine. It does **not** claim that every variety is classified, nor that Mori worked without Kawamata, Kollár, Reid, Shokurov, and others. It explains slogans carefully: ample tangent bundles and Hartshorne’s conjecture, extremal rays, flips versus divisorial contractions, and how [Birkar’s later boundedness chapter]({{ site.baseurl }}/contents/en/chapter02/02_10_Birkar_Algebraic_Geometry/) sits on this foundation.

- Distinguish a **divisorial contraction** from a **flip**, and say why flips are the hard step.
- Explain why Hironaka could call the three-fold theory “finished” as a *program* without listing every threefold up to isomorphism.

## 1. Why dimension three was a quantum jump

In dimension one, smooth projective curves are classified by genus. In dimension two, Enriques–Kodaira theory organizes surfaces by Kodaira dimension after contractions of exceptional curves. Hironaka’s ICM text insists that passing to dimension three was not “one more case.” New phenomena appear: small contractions whose exceptional locus has codimension at least two, singularities that must be allowed if the process is to continue, and birational maps that cannot be written as a sequence of blow-downs.

Mori’s answer was not a larger table of isomorphism types. It was a **directed process**. Given a (mildly singular) projective threefold $$X$$, look at the cone of curves. If the canonical class $$K_X$$ is already nef, stop: one has a minimal model. If not, an **extremal ray** on which $$K_X$$ is negative produces a contraction. Sometimes that contraction is divisorial. Sometimes it is small, and one must **flip**.

**Slogan.** Classification becomes an algorithm of elementary birational steps, not an encyclopedia of shapes.

---

## 2. Hartshorne’s conjecture and bend-and-break

Before the three-fold program, Mori proved (1979) a conjecture of Hartshorne: if $$X$$ is a smooth projective variety over an algebraically closed field and the tangent bundle $$T_X$$ is ample, then

$$
X\simeq\mathbb{P}^{\dim X}.
$$

Projective space has ample tangent bundle; the theorem says nothing else does. The method—**bend and break**—reduces to positive characteristic, deforms rational curves, and produces curves of smaller degree until a line appears. The same philosophy, that negativity of $$K_X$$ manufactures rational curves, became the engine of the cone theorem: the $$K_X$$-negative part of the cone of curves is generated by finitely many extremal rays, each spanned by a rational curve.

**Accuracy.** A 1977 note with Sumihiro treated special low-dimensional cases. The 1979 *Annals* paper is the theorem the community cites as Hartshorne’s conjecture in full.

---

## 3. Contractions and the missing flip

An extremal contraction $$\varphi:X\to Z$$ associated to a $$K_X$$-negative ray may:

- contract a divisor (a **divisorial contraction**), dropping the Picard number by one in a transparent way; or
- contract a small locus (curves, not a divisor). Then there is no morphism that simply “blows the locus down and stops.” One must construct a new threefold $$X^+$$ with a small map $$X^+\to Z$$ so that $$K_{X^+}$$ is positive on the flipped curves.

That replacement is a **flip**. Existence of flips in dimension three was the obstruction that kept the program from being a theorem. Mori’s 1988 paper in the *Journal of the American Mathematical Society*—“Flip theorem and the existence of minimal models for 3-folds”—supplied the missing step and, with the earlier cone and contraction theorems, gave existence of minimal models (or Mori fiber spaces) for threefolds.

A **Mori fiber space** is a fibration $$X\to Z$$ whose general fiber is Fano-like ($$-K$$ ample on fibers). It is the “positive” stopping point when Kodaira dimension is negative in the appropriate sense.

---

## 4. What “finished in dimension three” does and does not mean

Hironaka emphasized that the program was **constructive**: a general birational map of threefolds factors, in principle, into elementary pieces—divisorial contractions, flips, and flops—that one can hope to describe. That is a classification architecture. It is not a list of every smooth threefold up to isomorphism, and it is not a claim that rationality questions, explicit Fano lists, or singular cases become trivial.

It is also not a claim about **all dimensions**. Higher-dimensional flips, finite generation of canonical rings, and boundedness of Fano varieties required decades more. The collaborative **Birkar–Cascini–Hacon–McKernan** work and [Birkar’s Fano boundedness]({{ site.baseurl }}/contents/en/chapter02/02_10_Birkar_Algebraic_Geometry/) (Fields 2018) are later chapters of the same book. Mori opened the book and wrote the three-fold volume; he did not close the library.

---

## 5. Credit around the machine

Mori initiated extremal-ray theory and finished three-fold flips. The language of singularities (terminal, canonical), the cone theorem in analytic and algebraic settings, and many structural results were developed with Kawamata, Kollár, Reid, Shokurov, and others. Kollár–Mori’s later textbook *Birational Geometry of Algebraic Varieties* is the standard graduate companion. When you write a seminar paragraph, name the laboratory, not a lone hero who “invented birational geometry.”

---

## 6. Why a Fields Medal

Three interlocking reasons:

1. **Difficulty.** Three-fold flips had no surface analogue; constructing $$X^+$$ required a new birational calculus.
2. **Centrality.** Once dimension three runs, higher-dimensional MMP has a model of what a complete program looks like.
3. **Clarity of slogan with depth of method.** “Run contractions and flips until $$K$$ is nef, or a Fano fibration appears” is a sentence a graduate student can remember; the 1988 flip paper is a monument of algebraic geometry.

## Common confusions

| Claim | Correction |
|-------|------------|
| “Mori classified all algebraic varieties.” | He completed a birational program in dimension three; higher dimensions remained open. |
| “Hartshorne’s conjecture is about ample canonical bundles.” | It is about an **ample tangent bundle**, which forces projective space. |
| “A flip is a blow-up.” | A flip replaces a small locus; it is not an ordinary blow-down of a divisor. |
| “Minimal model means smooth minimal surface.” | In dimension three, mild singularities are part of the theory. |
| “Birkar’s medal replaces Mori’s.” | Birkar is a later higher-dimensional boundedness / MMP chapter built on this foundation. |
