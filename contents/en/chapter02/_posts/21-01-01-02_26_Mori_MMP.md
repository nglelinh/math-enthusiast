If you were asked to classify every shape in algebraic geometry, where would you begin? In dimension one (complex curves / Riemann surfaces), the census is astonishingly neat: everything is governed by one integer, the genus $$g$$ (sphere $$g=0$$, torus $$g=1$$, multi-holed surfaces $$g \ge 2$$). In dimension two (algebraic surfaces), the classic Italian school—Castelnuovo and Enriques, later refined by Kodaira and Zariski—still found a clean trimming procedure: keep contracting redundant $$(-1)$$-curves until you land on a smooth **minimal model**. Yet for half a century, the moment geometers stepped up to **dimension three**, the entire classical machine jammed: simple contractions were no longer enough, unavoidable mild singularities appeared, and algebraic threefolds looked like an untamable wilderness.

Right in the heart of that wilderness, **Shigefumi Mori** blazed the trail known today as the **Minimal Model Program (MMP)**—or **Mori’s program**—earning the **Fields Medal 1990** at ICM Kyoto. As **Heisuke Hironaka** recorded in his ICM lecture, the medal celebrates two deeply intertwined triumphs: Mori’s proof of **Hartshorne’s conjecture** (showing projective space $$\mathbb{P}^n$$ is the *only* smooth projective variety with an ample tangent bundle) via his revolutionary "bend-and-break" method, and his **1988 threefold flip theorem**—the capstone that turned 3-dimensional MMP into a completed theorem. This essay sets Mori’s foundation in its proper place while keeping clear the higher-dimensional sequels by Kawamata, Kollár, Reid, Shokurov, **BCHM**, and [Caucher Birkar]({{ site.baseurl }}/contents/en/chapter02/02_10_Birkar_Algebraic_Geometry/).

**Lesson roadmap:** Key ideas (birational geometry, canonical class $$K_X$$, terminal singularities) → Hand-verifiable worked examples (computing $$K_X = (d-5)H$$ on hypersurfaces in $$\mathbb{P}^4$$ & picturing codimension-2 surgery via the Atiyah flop) → Hartshorne’s conjecture & bend-and-break → Explanation: 4 steps of the 3-fold MMP (cone theorem, three contractions, 1988 flips, termination) → Boundary with higher dimensions (BCHM & Birkar).

## Key ideas: What Do Birational Geometry and the Minimal Model Program Study?

### 1. Birational equivalence—trimming redundant geometry
Two algebraic varieties are **birational** if they become isomorphic after discarding lower-dimensional subsets (points or curves). The elementary modification is a **blow-up**: replacing a point on a surface with a projective line $$\mathbb{P}^1$$ recording all tangent directions into that point. Because one can blow up endlessly, birational geometry asks the reverse question: *inside every birational equivalence class, can we strip away all excess to reach a simplest canonical representative—a **minimal model**?*

### 2. The canonical class $$K_X$$—algebraic curvature
On a complex curve $$C$$ of genus $$g$$, the canonical divisor has degree $$\deg(K_C) = 2g - 2$$:
- $$g = 0$$ ($$\mathbb{P}^1$$, positive curvature): $$\deg(K_C) = -2 < 0$$ ($$-K_C$$ ample—**Fano**);
- $$g = 1$$ (torus, flat): $$\deg(K_C) = 0$$ (**Calabi–Yau**);
- $$g \ge 2$$ (hyperbolic, negative curvature): $$\deg(K_C) = 2g - 2 > 0$$ ($$K_C$$ ample—**general type**).

In higher dimensions, we test the sign of $$K_X$$ via intersection numbers $$K_X \cdot C$$ with curves $$C \subset X$$. We call $$K_X$$ **nef** (*numerically effective*) if $$K_X \cdot C \ge 0$$ for *every* curve $$C$$—the hallmark of a **minimal model**.

### 3. Why dimension 3 forces terminal singularities and flips
On a surface, contracting a $$(-1)$$-curve keeps the surface smooth. In a **threefold**, a curve $$C \cong \mathbb{P}^1$$ has codimension $$2$$ (a thin thread floating in 3D space): contracting it directly destroys the $$\mathbb{Q}$$-Cartier property of $$K_Y$$ so intersection numbers break down! Therefore, 3-fold MMP must both allow mild **terminal singularities** (Miles Reid) and perform a codimension-2 surgery—a **flip**—replacing $$C$$ (where $$K_X \cdot C < 0$$) by a new curve $$C^+$$ (where $$K_{X^+} \cdot C^+ > 0$$).

## 1. Hand-Verifiable Worked Examples: Hypersurfaces in $$\mathbb{P}^4$$ and Codimension-2 Surgery

### Example 1: Classifying smooth hypersurfaces $$X_d \subset \mathbb{P}^4$$ via $$K_{X_d} = (d - 5)H$$
Let $$X_d \subset \mathbb{P}^4$$ be a smooth 3-dimensional hypersurface defined by a homogeneous polynomial of degree $$d$$. Since $$K_{\mathbb{P}^4} = -5H$$ (where $$H$$ is the hyperplane class), the adjunction formula immediately gives:

$$
K_{X_d} = (-5 + d)H = (d - 5)H.
$$

Plug in $$d = 1, 3, 5, 6$$ to watch the three great worlds of algebraic geometry appear on cue:

| Degree $$d$$ | Canonical class $$K_{X_d}$$ | Sign of $$K_{X_d}\cdot C$$ | Threefold class | MMP outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $$d = 1$$ ($$\mathbb{P}^3$$) | $$-4H$$ | Strongly negative ($$T_X$$ ample!) | Projective space (Mori 1979) | Mori fiber space over a point |
| $$d = 3$$ (cubic) | $$-2H$$ | Negative ($$-K_X$$ ample) | **Fano threefold** (covered by $$\mathbb{P}^1$$) | Mori fiber space |
| $$d = 5$$ (quintic) | $$0$$ | Zero (nef) | **Calabi–Yau threefold** (string theory) | Already a **minimal model** |
| $$d \ge 6$$ | $$(d-5)H$$ | Positive ($$K_X$$ ample) | **General type** | Already the unique **minimal model** |

### Example 2: Visualizing codimension-2 surgery (the Atiyah flop)
Consider the singular 3-fold cone $$Y = \{xy - zw = 0\} \subset \mathbb{C}^4$$ (singular at the origin). One can resolve the origin in **two distinct small ways**:
- *Resolution $$X$$:* set $$x/z = w/y = \lambda \in \mathbb{P}^1$$, replacing the origin by a curve $$C \cong \mathbb{P}^1$$.
- *Resolution $$X^+$$:* set $$x/w = z/y = \mu \in \mathbb{P}^1$$, replacing the origin by a *different* curve $$C^+ \cong \mathbb{P}^1$$!

Outside the threads $$C$$ and $$C^+$$, $$X$$ and $$X^+$$ are identical—we pulled out $$C$$ and threaded in $$C^+$$ orthogonally! In this symmetric model (**Atiyah flop**), $$K_X \cdot C = 0$$; in a **Mori flip**, the surrounding singularity is asymmetric so the sign of $$K$$ genuinely flips from $$K_X \cdot C < 0$$ to $$K_{X^+} \cdot C^+ > 0$$!

## 2. Hartshorne’s Conjecture and Bend-and-Break (1979)

Around 1970, **Robin Hartshorne** conjectured that any smooth projective variety $$X$$ with **ample tangent bundle $$T_X$$** (much stronger than Fano $$-K_X = \det T_X$$ ample) must be $$\mathbb{P}^n$$. (Its differential-geometric twin, Frankel’s conjecture, was proved by **Siu–Yau** in 1980 using harmonic maps.)

In **Mori’s 1979 theorem** (*Ann. of Math.* 110), Mori proved Hartshorne’s conjecture purely algebraically and unveiled **bend-and-break**:
- If a curve $$C$$ passing through a fixed point $$x \in X$$ has enough deformation freedom (guaranteed by positivity of $$T_X$$ or $$-K_X$$), we "bend" it into a one-parameter family still pinned at $$x$$.
- By reducing modulo a prime $$p > 0$$ to harness the Frobenius morphism—an inspired algebraic trick—Mori proved the family must **break** in the limit into lower-degree pieces, spawning at least one **rational curve** $$\mathbb{P}^1$$ through $$x$$!
- Hence every Fano manifold is **uniruled** (covered by rational curves), and ample $$T_X$$ locks the geometry of minimal rational curves through $$x$$ so tightly that $$X \simeq \mathbb{P}^n$$!

*(This characterizes $$\mathbb{P}^n$$; the full census of smooth Fano threefolds with $$B_2 \ge 2$$ is the later **Mori–Mukai** classification, 1981.)*

## 3. Explanation: Running the 3-Dimensional Minimal Model Program

How does the MMP reduce an arbitrary algebraic threefold step by step? Follow four stages:

#### Step 1: The Cone Theorem finds the negative directions of $$K_X$$
Consider the closed cone of curves $$\overline{\mathrm{NE}}(X)$$. If $$K_X$$ is already nef ($$K_X \cdot C \ge 0$$ for all $$C$$), **stop**: $$X$$ is a minimal model! Otherwise, the **Cone Theorem** (Mori 1982; generalized by Kawamata, Kollár, Reid, Shokurov) proves that the $$K_X < 0$$ half of the cone is **locally polyhedral**, spanned by discrete **extremal rays** $$R_i$$—each generated by a rational curve produced by bend-and-break!

#### Step 2: The Contraction Theorem splits into three cases
For an extremal ray $$R$$ with $$K_X \cdot R < 0$$, the Contraction Theorem constructs a morphism $$\varphi: X \to Y$$ contracting exactly the curves in $$R$$:
- **Fiber-type contraction ($$\dim Y < \dim X$$):** general fibers are Fano $$\Rightarrow$$ **stop**, you have reached a **Mori fiber space**!
- **Divisorial contraction:** a surface (divisor) is contracted, Picard number drops by $$1$$ $$\Rightarrow$$ replace $$X$$ by $$Y$$ and loop back to Step 1.
- **Small contraction:** only finitely many curves (codimension 2) are contracted $$\Rightarrow$$ go to Step 3 (**flip**)!

#### Step 3: Mori’s 3-Fold Flip Theorem (*J. Amer. Math. Soc.*, 1988)
This was the final wall blocking 3-fold MMP, and Mori broke through it solo: for any small contraction of a projective threefold with $$\mathbb{Q}$$-factorial terminal singularities, **the flip $$\varphi^+: X^+ \to Y$$ exists**, flips the sign of $$K$$ to positive on the new curve, and preserves $$\mathbb{Q}$$-factorial terminal singularities!

#### Step 4: Termination of flips (Shokurov’s difficulty)
Since flips do not lower the Picard number, could flips repeat forever? **Vyacheslav Shokurov (1985)** invented a non-negative integer invariant—the **difficulty** of 3-fold terminal singularities—and proved it **strictly drops** after every flip! Thus any sequence of 3-fold flips terminates in finitely many steps, guaranteeing a minimal model or Mori fiber space for every terminal $$\mathbb{Q}$$-factorial threefold!

Summary of the 3-fold MMP loop:

$$
X \text{ (3D, } K_X \text{ not nef)}
\;\xrightarrow{\text{Cone Theorem}}\;
\text{Extremal ray } R
\;\xrightarrow{\text{Divisorial / Flip (Mori 1988)}}\;
\text{Minimal model } (K \text{ nef}) \text{ or Mori fiber space}.
$$

## 4. Boundary with Higher Dimensions and Why a Fields Medal

**Do not say:** Mori finished MMP in all dimensions. Higher-dimensional flips remained open for two more decades until **Birkar–Cascini–Hacon–McKernan (BCHM, 2010)** for varieties of general type and [Birkar’s]({{ site.baseurl }}/contents/en/chapter02/02_10_Birkar_Algebraic_Geometry/) boundedness of Fano varieties (Fields 2018); general termination of flips in dimension $$\ge 4$$ remains subtle and open.

**Why a Fields Medal 1990:** Mori’s Hartshorne proof gifted algebraic geometry the bend-and-break engine; his 1988 flip theorem completed the 3-fold Minimal Model Program; and together they laid the entire foundation for modern higher-dimensional birational geometry. (Mori later served as the first East Asian President of the IMU.)

## Common confusions

| Claim | Correction |
|-------|------------|
| “Mori finished MMP in all dimensions.” | He completed the 3-fold program (flips in dimension 3). Higher dimensions came later (BCHM and others). |
| “Ample tangent bundle means Fano.” | Fano is ampleness of $$-K_X=\det T_X$$, which is weaker; ample $$T_X$$ forces $$\mathbb{P}^n$$. |
| “A flip is a blow-up.” | A flip replaces a small locus; it is not a classical blow-up of a divisor. |
| “Minimal model means smooth minimal surface.” | In dimension $$\ge 3$$, minimal models are allowed terminal singularities; $$K$$ nef is the goal. |
| “Hartshorne’s conjecture is still open.” | Mori proved it (1979 *Annals*). |
| “Birkar’s medal replaces Mori’s.” | Birkar extends the higher-dimensional architecture; the 3-fold foundation remains Mori’s. |
| “The cone theorem is only Mori’s.” | Mori’s 3-fold cone is foundational; Kawamata–Kollár–Reid–Shokurov developed the general/log package. |
