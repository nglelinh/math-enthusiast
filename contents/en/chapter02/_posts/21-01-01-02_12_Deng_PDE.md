**Yu Deng** (University of Chicago) received the **Fields Medal 2026** at ICM Philadelphia, alongside Hong Wang, Jacob Tsimerman, and John Pardon. The medal recognizes PDE work that turns a century-old dream into theorems: when—and how—can a system with **vastly many interacting pieces** be replaced, rigorously, by a **simpler statistical equation**?

**IMU short citation (official):**  
For his work in partial differential equations, including the rigorous derivation of the Boltzmann equation from hard-sphere dynamics for rarefied gases, the derivation of wave kinetic equations from nonlinear dispersive systems, and probabilistic approaches to nonlinear Schrödinger dynamics.

This essay is for learners who live between **applied fluid models** and **pure analysis**. It starts from everyday simulation choices (resolution, Reynolds number, DNS/LES/RANS), climbs to Hilbert’s sixth problem, and explains—without fake software claims—why Deng–Hani–Ma and Deng’s wave-kinetic program deserve attention.

## Learning objectives

After this lecture you should be able to:

- Describe three nested levels of gas description: **particles**, **Boltzmann kinetics**, **fluid equations**.
- Explain **molecular chaos** and why **recollisions** threaten long-time derivations of Boltzmann’s equation.
- State Lanford’s short-time landmark and what Deng–Hani–Ma advance (long-time control in rarefied hard-sphere gas, under regularity of the Boltzmann solution).
- Sketch the parallel story for **wave kinetic equations** from nonlinear dispersive systems.
- Distinguish **using** multi-scale models effectively from **proving** they emerge.
- Avoid overclaiming: this is not a new weather code, and it does not close the Millennium Navier–Stokes regularity problem.

**Prerequisites.** Calculus-level PDEs as “evolution laws”; probability density as a description of many particles. No prior kinetic theory course required.

**Seminar links.** LO1 (hard open-adjacent programs); LO4 (classical continuum models ↔ microscopic mechanisms); pair with [Navier–Stokes problem]({{ site.baseurl }}/contents/en/chapter01/01_05_Navier_Stokes/) and [Mathematics of AI]({{ site.baseurl }}/contents/en/chapter06/06_02_Mathematics_of_AI/) only for multi-scale *analogy*, not identity.

## 1. Why resolution matters: traffic, mosquitoes, and air in a room

When you simulate a city traffic system, the ideal would be to track every car: position, velocity, interactions. For a swarm of insects, every insect. That is high **resolution**—and astronomically expensive if the domain is a whole city or a migration of millions of animals.

Often one switches to **aggregate** description: flow rate and average speed of traffic; density of a swarm. Detail is discarded so the large scale becomes computable.

A gas in a room is the same story at molecular scale:

- **Finest level.** Enormous numbers of molecules fly and collide. Conceptually “clean”: Newton’s laws for hard spheres (tiny billiard balls). In principle, knowing every position and velocity determines the future. In practice: tens or hundreds of billions of particles make full tracking both impossible and unnecessary if you only want room temperature, pressure, or a fan-driven draft.
- **Kinetic level.** Boltzmann’s equation tracks a **probability density** of particles in position–velocity space, with a collision operator. You stop asking where *this* molecule is and ask how the cloud of states evolves.
- **Fluid level.** The gas is a continuum field: density, bulk velocity, pressure, temperature. The classical tools are **Euler** and **Navier–Stokes–Fourier** equations—the workhorses of aerodynamics, weather, HVAC, and environmental flow.

![Particle → Boltzmann → fluid]({{ site.baseurl }}/img/chapter_img/deng_scales_hierarchy.svg)

*Figure. Nested descriptions of a gas. Each arrow is a limiting story that must be justified.*

That ladder sits behind weather forecasting, aircraft design, building ventilation, air-pollution models, flood modelling, and lake temperature calculations. Engineers use it daily. Mathematics asks: **under which hypotheses is the ladder a theorem, not only a custom?**

## 2. Turbulence modelling: DNS, LES, RANS as deliberate loss of detail

In turbulent flow simulation the same philosophy appears with famous acronyms.

**Reynolds number** measures the relative strength of inertia versus viscosity. Honey creeping has low Re (orderly layers). Water through a dam, air around a jet, or a large river has high Re: inertia dominates, flow becomes disordered, and eddies cascade—large structures break into smaller ones until viscosity dissipates energy as heat. The range between largest and smallest eddies widens rapidly with Re.

- **DNS (Direct Numerical Simulation).** Solve Navier–Stokes finely enough to resolve essentially the full cascade. Extreme cost: grids with hundreds of millions of cells, thousands of CPUs, days or weeks of wall time even for a tiny channel of a few centimeters—plus environmental cost of supercomputing. Used when small-scale physics truly matters (medical devices, micro heat exchange, near-bullet aerodynamics, …).
- **LES (Large Eddy Simulation).** Resolve large eddies; model or discard subgrid scales. Less accurate in principle; usable for car pressure fields, high-speed aircraft, larger domains.
- **RANS (Reynolds-averaged Navier–Stokes).** Average the turbulence; close Reynolds stresses with models. Workhorse for rivers, storms, operational forecasting where full eddy resolution is hopeless.

![DNS / LES / RANS]({{ site.baseurl }}/img/chapter_img/deng_dns_les_rans.svg)

*Figure. Each step throws away detail and hopes a statistical stand-in is good enough.*

**Pedagogical moral.** Every multi-scale model is a **decision about what to forget**. Deng’s work does not replace your turbulence closure. It clarifies a deeper cousin of that decision: forgetting particle identity in favor of kinetic or fluid laws must be *controlled*, or correlations from the past will poison the statistics.

## 3. Hilbert’s sixth problem: a mathematical bridge, not a software patch

In 1900, **David Hilbert** asked (among other things) for a rigorous path from atomistic mechanics through statistical laws to continuum equations of matter. Physicists and engineers long used Boltzmann and Navier–Stokes successfully. **Using** and **proving** are different.

A central obstruction is **recollision** and the breakdown of **molecular chaos**.

Boltzmann’s equation leans on the idea that, just before a collision, two particles are essentially uncorrelated—their joint law nearly factors. Over time, a particle can meet an “old acquaintance” again, or share history through a chain of intermediaries. Correlations accumulate. The bookkeeping of possible collision histories branches like a **forest**.

Imagine a crowded party: at the start, most guests are independent strangers. Later A talks to B, B introduces C, C returns to A—the social graph is entangled. You can no longer pretend everyone is independent.

![Recollision forest]({{ site.baseurl }}/img/chapter_img/deng_recollision_forest.svg)

*Figure. Collision histories branch; long-time control is combinatorial as well as analytic.*

In 1975, **Oscar Lanford** proved a Newtonian hard-sphere system converges to Boltzmann’s equation—but only on a **very short time** interval. After that, the forest of histories overwhelms classical estimates.

## 4. Deng–Hani–Ma: managing the forest for long times

With **Zaher Hani** and **Xiao Ma**, Deng completed a central link of the kinetic program for a **rarefied gas of elastically colliding hard spheres**:

- Encode and track full collision histories with combinatorial branching structures.
- Develop a method to **cut** oversized structures into smaller pieces that can be estimated.
- Prove that, in a rarefied limit, correlations generated by recollisions remain small enough that **Boltzmann continues to describe the particle system for arbitrarily long times**, provided the corresponding Boltzmann solution stays **regular**.

In follow-up work, the group connects this to existing hydrodynamic limits to derive, from the hard-sphere system, compressible **Euler** and incompressible **Navier–Stokes–Fourier** fluid equations in appropriate regimes.

**Precision about Hilbert 6.** They solve a **specific, major piece** of the kinetic pathway in Hilbert’s sixth problem—from Newton’s laws through Boltzmann toward fluid equations—for hard spheres under stated hypotheses. They do **not** close every question historically packed under the broad slogan “Hilbert’s sixth problem,” and they do not settle every regime of kinetic theory.

**A human detail.** Accounts of the work note an idea about breaking long interaction histories into manageable segments coming to Deng in an ordinary moment (a Korean fried-chicken restaurant)—a reminder that conceptual cuts in infinite combinatorial trees can start as very human sketches.

## 5. From gas particles to waves: a parallel bridge

Deng’s toolbox is not only hard spheres. Much of it grew in **wave turbulence** and nonlinear dispersive PDE.

Drop many pebbles on a pond: circular waves meet, interfere, and exchange energy. Oceans are far richer—wind, tides, bathymetry, stratification create surface and internal waves that transport heat, oxygen, nutrients, and carbon. Global ocean models cannot resolve every small wave; they need **statistical** laws for energy spectra and transfers across scales.

In a weakly nonlinear wave field, energy is exchanged among many modes through resonant interactions. At the fine level one has a deterministic nonlinear wave equation. At the coarse level one wants a **wave kinetic equation** for the averaged energy density in wavenumber space.

![Wave kinetic limit]({{ site.baseurl }}/img/chapter_img/deng_wave_kinetic.svg)

*Figure. Many-mode nonlinear waves → statistical transport of energy.*

**Deng and Hani** proved, in a precise limiting regime of cubic nonlinear Schrödinger dynamics, that detailed modal dynamics converge to a wave kinetic equation on the kinetic timescale, and extended justifications to longer times. Physics had used wave turbulence for decades; full mathematical emergence of the kinetic equation from a nonlinear dispersive system of this type had not been available.

For an applied researcher of internal waves, stratification, and mixing: the theorems do **not** certify a full realistic ocean wave-kinetic model (rotation, variable stratification, topography, wave breaking, strong interactions remain outside). They underwrite the **core mathematical question**: how can deterministic multi-wave interactions produce a clean statistical energy-transport law?

Deng’s broader portfolio also touches gravity–capillary waves, two-dimensional gravity waves, and long-time instability phenomena (e.g. Couette-related questions)—flow, waves, probability, and statistical physics as one toolkit, not isolated silos.

## 6. Will we have new weather and flood software tomorrow?

**Honest answer: not tomorrow, and not as a drop-in package.**

Foundational theorems rarely install as a new RANS closure. Their value travels a longer path:

1. They show that scale transitions are not mere computational tricks: under clear hypotheses, large-scale equations **can** emerge from small-scale dynamics.
2. They support the mindset of multi-scale modelling: use particle or kinetic resolution only where microscopic effects matter; use cheaper continuum laws elsewhere—**when the hypotheses match**.
3. They isolate the universal obstacle of reduction: **correlations and memory do not vanish for free.** After averaging or filtering, discarded small-scale motion returns as a **closure problem**—exactly the daily pain of turbulence modelling.

Deng’s results do not solve DNS/LES/RANS closure. Their control of long interaction histories may still inspire how we think about the birth of statistical laws in complex nonlinear systems.

**Application at the deepest layer.** An engineer need not reprove Boltzmann-from-Newton each morning. But the **reliability and domain of validity** of continuum tools depend on such foundations standing firm. Every lake or river model still requires choices of grid, time step, scheme, and turbulence model—always forgetting some of nature to keep what the question needs. Deng’s work is a reminder that those acts of forgetting are profound, not trivial.

## 7. Why a Fields Medal

The Fields citation clusters three contributions:

1. Rigorous derivation of **Boltzmann** from hard-sphere dynamics in rarefied gases (with collaborators, long-time theory).
2. Derivation of **wave kinetic equations** from nonlinear dispersive systems.
3. **Probabilistic methods** for nonlinear Schrödinger dynamics.

Bigger than a single estimate: a new way to control **accumulation of correlations** in systems with countless interactions—and to complete a bridge that had a famous gap since Lanford’s short-time theorem.

For non-mathematicians, the payoff is not a new chip. Modern science runs on foundation layers. Applied mathematics at its deepest does not always forecast tomorrow’s flood; it explains **why the equations used to forecast floods have a right to exist**.

## 8. An open door still ahead: Navier–Stokes regularity

A separate Millennium Prize problem asks whether 3D incompressible Navier–Stokes solutions always remain smooth for reasonable data, or can form singularities in finite time. That is **not** settled by Deng’s kinetic derivations. It remains a flagship open problem for future generations—possibly with help from analysis, computation, and carefully used AI as a search tool for structure, never a substitute for proof.

See also this course’s [Navier–Stokes problem essay]({{ site.baseurl }}/contents/en/chapter01/01_05_Navier_Stokes/).

## Common confusions

| Claim | Correction |
|-------|------------|
| “Hilbert’s sixth problem is fully solved.” | A major kinetic hard-sphere link is advanced; the historical package of “sixth problem” is broader. |
| “Boltzmann is now free of all hypotheses.” | Results require rarefaction, regularity of Boltzmann solutions, and precise limits. |
| “This gives a new LES model for my CFD code.” | No: foundational emergence theorems, not a drop-in closure. |
| “Wave kinetic theorems solve ocean internal waves.” | They justify kinetic limits in mathematical regimes; oceans add physics outside those regimes. |
| “Lanford already did long-time Boltzmann.” | Lanford is a short-time landmark; long-time control is the Deng–Hani–Ma leap. |
