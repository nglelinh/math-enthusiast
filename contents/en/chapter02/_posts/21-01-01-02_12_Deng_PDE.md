Take a deep breath. Inside the single liter of air in front of your face, roughly $$2.5 \times 10^{22}$$ gas molecules are zipping around at hundreds of meters per second and colliding billions of times every second according to Newton’s **time-reversible** laws of motion. Yet when engineers design an airplane wing or meteorologists forecast a typhoon, they never track a single molecule—they write down smooth, **irreversible** continuum laws like the **Boltzmann equation** and the **Navier–Stokes equations**.

In 1900, David Hilbert challenged mathematicians (his famous **Sixth Problem**) to turn that physicist’s leap of faith into a rigorous theorem: *prove mathematically that microscopic Newtonian collisions actually produce Boltzmann’s statistical law and macroscopic fluid mechanics.* For over a century, the problem resisted every assault—even Oscar Lanford’s celebrated 1975 theorem broke down after a tiny fraction of a single mean collision time.

At ICM 2026 in Philadelphia, **Yu Deng** (University of Chicago) received the **Fields Medal 2026** (alongside Hong Wang, Jacob Tsimerman, and John Pardon) for cracking this century-old barrier—both for **hard-sphere gases** (with Zaher Hani and Xiao Ma) and for **nonlinear wave turbulence** (with Zaher Hani).

**IMU short citation (official):**  
For his work in partial differential equations, including the rigorous derivation of the Boltzmann equation from hard-sphere dynamics for rarefied gases, the derivation of wave kinetic equations from nonlinear dispersive systems, and probabilistic approaches to nonlinear Schrödinger dynamics.

This essay bridges everyday engineering intuition (resolution, Reynolds number, DNS/LES/RANS) with pure PDE analysis—complete with concrete numerical scaling checks—to show why Deng’s control of **recollision forests** is one of the defining breakthroughs of modern analysis.

## Key ideas: What Fields Power Kinetic PDEs?

Yu Deng’s work sits at the thrilling intersection of three areas that bridge physics, probability, and pure analysis.

### 1. Partial Differential Equations (PDEs) & Fluid Mechanics
**What is it?** PDEs describe how continuous fields—temperature, fluid velocity, wave amplitude—evolve in space and time.

**What does it study?**
- **Dispersive PDEs (like Nonlinear Schrödinger, NLS):** Waves of different frequencies travel at different speeds and exchange energy through nonlinear interactions.
- **Hydrodynamic equations (Euler, Navier–Stokes–Fourier):** Continuum equations governing air, oceans, and plasmas.

### 2. Kinetic Theory & Statistical Mechanics: The Bridge Between Micro and Macro
**What is it?** Kinetic theory occupies the middle floor between $$10^{23}$$ colliding atoms (microscopic Newton’s laws) and smooth fluids (macroscopic Navier–Stokes). Instead of tracking individual particles, it tracks a single **phase-space density** $$f(t, x, v)$$—the probability of finding a particle at position $$x$$ with velocity $$v$$ at time $$t$$.

**What does it study?**
- **Boltzmann’s equation:** Governs $$f(t,x,v)$$ via free streaming plus a quadratic collision operator.
- **The arrow of time (irreversibility):** Newton’s laws run identically forward and backward in time, yet Boltzmann’s entropy strictly increases! Explaining how irreversibility emerges rigorously in the $$N \to \infty$$ limit is one of the deepest conceptual puzzles in mathematical physics.

### 3. Combinatorics of Feynman Diagrams & Probabilistic PDEs
**What is it?** When you expand a many-particle or many-wave system over time, every interaction splits into a branching tree (or graph) of past collisions—closely resembling **Feynman diagrams** in quantum physics.

**What does it study?**
- **Diagrammatic control:** Counting how many distinct collision histories exist, separating "clean" tree-like histories (molecular chaos) from tangled "recollision" loops, and proving that tangled loops carry vanishing total probability in the scaling limit.

## 1. Why resolution matters: traffic, mosquitoes, and air in a room

When you simulate city traffic, tracking every single car is high resolution—and astronomically expensive across an entire metropolis. Engineers therefore switch to **aggregate** variables: traffic flow rate and average speed.

A gas in a room lives on a three-story ladder of resolutions:

- **Finest level (Newtonian hard spheres).** Enormous numbers $$N$$ of tiny billiard balls of diameter $$\varepsilon$$ fly straight and bounce elastically. Deterministic and time-reversible—yet impossible to track individually when $$N \sim 10^{23}$$.
- **Middle level (Boltzmann kinetics).** Tracks the probability density $$f(t,x,v)$$ in position–velocity space with a collision integral.
- **Coarsest level (Fluid continuum).** Tracks bulk density, velocity, pressure, and temperature via **Euler** and **Navier–Stokes–Fourier** equations.

![Particle → Boltzmann → fluid]({{ site.baseurl }}/img/chapter_img/deng_scales_hierarchy.svg)

*Figure. Nested descriptions of a gas. Each arrow is a limiting story that must be justified.*

### Hand-Verifiable Example 1: The Boltzmann–Grad Scaling Limit
To derive Boltzmann’s equation cleanly, how small must the particle diameter $$\varepsilon$$ be as the particle count $$N \to \infty$$? You want a gas that is **dilute** (almost empty space) yet still experiences **$$O(1)$$ collisions per particle per second**!

A sphere of diameter $$\varepsilon$$ sweeping through unit distance carves out a collision cylinder of cross-sectional area $$\sim \varepsilon^2$$. In a unit box containing $$N$$ spheres, the expected number of collisions per unit distance is proportional to $$N\varepsilon^2$$. So the **Boltzmann–Grad scaling** fixes

$$
N \varepsilon^2 = 1 \quad (\text{in 3D}).
$$

Plug in concrete numbers: take $$N = 10^{12}$$ spheres of diameter $$\varepsilon = 10^{-6}$$ in a $$1\times 1\times 1$$ box. Check two numbers by hand:
1. **Collision rate:** $$N\varepsilon^2 = 10^{12} \times (10^{-6})^2 = 1$$ — every particle travels an $$O(1)$$ **mean free path** before hitting another sphere!
2. **Total volume occupied by all $$10^{12}$$ spheres:** Each sphere has radius $$\varepsilon/2 = 0.5\times 10^{-6}$$, so
   $$
   N \cdot \frac{4}{3}\pi \left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)^3
   = 10^{12} \cdot \frac{\pi}{6}\cdot 10^{-18}
   \approx 5.24 \times 10^{-7}
   $$
   (and even using radius $$\varepsilon$$ gives only $$\approx 4.19 \times 10^{-6}$$—more than $$99.999\%$$ empty space!).

That razor-thin regime—almost completely empty space, yet constant collision frequency—is the exact arena of Hilbert’s Sixth Problem for rarefied gases.

## 2. Turbulence modelling: DNS, LES, RANS as deliberate loss of detail

In turbulent fluid simulation, engineers face an everyday cousin of this scale ladder. **Reynolds number** measures inertia versus viscosity: honey has low Re (smooth layers); air around a jet has high Re (a wild cascade of eddies from meters down to millimeters).

- **DNS (Direct Numerical Simulation):** Resolves every tiny eddy directly. Accurate, yet brutally expensive—hundreds of millions of grid cells and weeks of supercomputing for a tiny channel.
- **LES (Large Eddy Simulation):** Resolves large eddies and models the small subgrid scales.
- **RANS (Reynolds-Averaged Navier–Stokes):** Averages out turbulence entirely and closes the equations with empirical models—the workhorse of weather and river forecasting.

![DNS / LES / RANS]({{ site.baseurl }}/img/chapter_img/deng_dns_les_rans.svg)

*Figure. Each step throws away detail and hopes a statistical stand-in is good enough.*

**Moral.** Every multi-scale model is a **decision about what to forget**. Deng’s work does not replace your turbulence closure—it illuminates the deepest mathematical version of that act of forgetting: proving that discarded microscopic correlations do not silently poison the macroscopic law.

## 3. Hilbert’s sixth problem and the factorial wall of Lanford (1975)

Boltzmann’s equation rests on **molecular chaos**: right before two spheres collide, their velocities are statistically independent. At time $$t=0$$, you can start particles independently. As time runs forward, however, particle A hits B, B hits C, and C later hits A—a **recollision**. Correlations accumulate, and the tree of possible collision histories branches like an overgrown **forest**.

![Recollision forest]({{ site.baseurl }}/img/chapter_img/deng_recollision_forest.svg)

*Figure. Collision histories branch; long-time control is combinatorial as well as analytic.*

In 1975, **Oscar Lanford** proved that hard spheres do converge to Boltzmann’s equation—but **only for a tiny short time** $$t < t_0$$ (roughly a fifth of a single mean collision time!). Why did Lanford’s proof hit a brick wall? Because when you trace $$n$$ collisions backward in time without smart pruning, the number of possible collision trees grows like a **factorial** $$n!$$, while the time-simplex integral only supplies a damping factor $$t^n / n!$$. The series $$\sum_n C^n t^n$$ converges **only** when $$C t < 1$$! For fifty years, nobody knew how to break past that $$n!$$ convergence barrier.

---

### Explanation: How Deng–Hani–Ma Tamed the Recollision Forest

How did **Yu Deng, Zaher Hani, and Xiao Ma** (2024–2025) break a 50-year-old barrier and prove convergence to Boltzmann for **arbitrarily long times** (as long as the Boltzmann solution stays smooth)? Follow the four-step breakthrough slowly:

#### Step 1: Slice long time into manageable layers instead of one giant expansion
Trying to expand from time $$t=0$$ straight to $$t=100$$ in a single Duhamel/BBGKY series explodes factorially. Deng–Hani–Ma broke the time interval $$[0, T]$$ into many small time layers $$[0, \tau], [\tau, 2\tau], \dots$$ and kept track of the **cumulants** (the exact discrepancy between the true correlated particle distribution and the factorized molecular-chaos distribution) at each layer boundary.

#### Step 2: Encode full collision histories as combinatorial "molecules"
Every interaction history is drawn as a directed graph ("molecule") whose vertices are collisions and whose edges are free-flying particle trajectories. A pure **tree** (no cycles) represents clean molecular chaos; any **cycle** represents a recollision that creates statistical memory.

#### Step 3: The cutting algorithm—chop giant graphs into controlled elementary pieces
Here is the conceptual heart of the proof (reportedly sketched by Deng during an ordinary meal at a Korean fried-chicken restaurant!). Instead of crudely bounding all $$n!$$ diagrams at once, Deng–Hani–Ma designed a surgical **cutting algorithm** that slices arbitrarily large, tangled collision graphs into elementary atomic pieces while preserving enough geometric constraints (from exact hard-sphere collision geometry in $$\mathbb{R}^3$$) to beat the combinatorial multiplicity!

#### Step 4: Keep recollision correlations vanishingly small up to any regular time $$T$$
With the combinatorial explosion tamed, every recollision cycle carries a tiny geometric probability factor (a power of $$\varepsilon$$). Summing across all time layers proves that **molecular chaos propagates for arbitrarily long times**—matching the full lifespan of the smooth Boltzmann solution! In follow-up work (2025), Deng–Hani–Ma chained this directly to classical hydrodynamic limits, delivering a rigorous bridge from **Newton’s hard spheres → Boltzmann → compressible Euler and incompressible Navier–Stokes–Fourier** in the rarefied regime.

In one schematic line:

$$
\text{Newtonian hard spheres }(N\varepsilon^2=1)
\;\xrightarrow{\;\text{layered cumulants + cutting algorithm}\;}\;
\text{Long-time Boltzmann}
\;\xrightarrow{\;\text{hydrodynamic limit}\;}\;
\text{Euler / Navier–Stokes–Fourier}.
$$

## 4. From gas particles to waves: a parallel bridge

Deng’s diagrammatic cutting philosophy was actually forged first in **wave turbulence** and nonlinear dispersive PDEs.

Drop many pebbles on a pond—or watch wind, tides, and ocean currents stir millions of interacting surface and internal waves. Global ocean models cannot track every ripple; they rely on a **wave kinetic equation** describing how wave energy flows statistically across frequencies (wavenumbers).

### Hand-Verifiable Example 2: 4-Wave Rectangular Resonance
In a cubic nonlinear Schrödinger (NLS) wave field, waves interact four at a time with wavevectors $$k_1, k_2, k_3, k_4 \in \mathbb{Z}^d$$. Energy transfers persistently only when **both momentum and dispersion energy are conserved** (a 4-wave resonance):

$$
k_1 + k_2 = k_3 + k_4,
\qquad
\lvert k_1\rvert^2 + \lvert k_2\rvert^2 = \lvert k_3\rvert^2 + \lvert k_4\rvert^2.
$$

Check a concrete 2D example by hand! Take the four corners of a rectangle:
$$
k_1 = (0,0), \quad k_2 = (2,3), \quad k_3 = (2,0), \quad k_4 = (0,3).
$$
- Momentum sum: $$k_1 + k_2 = (2,3) = (2,0) + (0,3) = k_3 + k_4$$. $$\checkmark$$
- Energy sum: $$\lvert k_1\rvert^2 + \lvert k_2\rvert^2 = 0 + (4+9) = 13 = 4 + 9 = \lvert k_3\rvert^2 + \lvert k_4\rvert^2$$. $$\checkmark$$

In fact, **every** rectangle in frequency space forms an exact 4-wave resonance!

![Wave kinetic limit]({{ site.baseurl }}/img/chapter_img/deng_wave_kinetic.svg)

*Figure. Many-mode nonlinear waves → statistical transport of energy.*

**Deng and Hani** proved, for the cubic nonlinear Schrödinger equation with random initial phases, that the deterministic multi-wave dynamics rigorously converge to the **wave kinetic equation** over the full kinetic timescale and beyond—turning a century-old physics heuristic (Peierls, Hasselmann, Zakharov) into a theorem.

For applied oceanographers studying internal waves and mixing: these theorems do not yet cover rotation, topography, or wave breaking, but they underwrite the **core mathematical mechanism** showing how deterministic wave resonance gives birth to statistical energy transport.

## 5. Will we have new weather and flood software tomorrow?

**Honest answer: not tomorrow, and not as a drop-in CFD plugin.**

Foundational theorems travel a deeper path:
1. They prove that scale transitions are not mere engineering luck: under clear hypotheses, macroscopic laws truly emerge from microscopic physics.
2. They sharpen multi-scale modeling instincts: use costly particle/kinetic solvers only where microscopic correlations matter, and continuum laws where hypotheses hold.
3. They isolate the universal mathematical obstacle behind every closure problem: **correlations and memory never disappear for free.**

Applied mathematics at its deepest does not always forecast tomorrow’s flood; it explains **why the equations used to forecast floods have a right to exist**.

## 6. Why a Fields Medal (and what stays open)

The 2026 Fields Medal citation clusters three triumphs:
1. Long-time rigorous derivation of **Boltzmann** (and fluid limits) from Newtonian hard spheres (Deng–Hani–Ma).
2. Rigorous derivation of **wave kinetic equations** from nonlinear dispersive PDEs (Deng–Hani).
3. **Probabilistic methods** for nonlinear Schrödinger dynamics (including random-data well-posedness).

**Still open: Millennium Navier–Stokes regularity.** Whether 3D incompressible Navier–Stokes solutions stay smooth forever or can blow up in finite time is **not** solved by Deng’s kinetic derivations—it remains a flagship open problem ([Navier–Stokes problem essay]({{ site.baseurl }}/contents/en/chapter01/01_05_Navier_Stokes/)).

## Common confusions

| Claim | Correction |
|-------|------------|
| “Hilbert’s sixth problem is fully solved.” | A major kinetic hard-sphere link is proved; the full historical scope of Hilbert’s sixth problem is broader. |
| “Boltzmann is now free of all hypotheses.” | Results require the Boltzmann–Grad rarefied limit and regularity of the limiting Boltzmann solution. |
| “This gives a new LES model for my CFD code.” | No: these are foundational emergence theorems, not a drop-in turbulence closure. |
| “Wave kinetic theorems solve ocean internal waves.” | They justify kinetic limits in idealized dispersive regimes; real oceans add physics outside those regimes. |
| “Lanford already did long-time Boltzmann.” | Lanford (1975) was a short-time landmark; long-time control past the $$n!$$ barrier is the Deng–Hani–Ma leap. |
