**Wendelin Werner** received the **Fields Medal 2006**

> “For his contributions to the development of stochastic Loewner evolution, the geometry of two-dimensional Brownian motion, and conformal field theory.”  
> — [IMU, Fields Medals 2006](https://www.mathunion.org/imu-awards/fields-medal/fields-medals-2006)

The citation does **not** say that Werner invented SLE. **Oded Schramm** introduced stochastic Loewner evolution in 2000 (*Israel J. Math.* 118, “Scaling limits of loop-erased random walks and uniform spanning trees”). Werner’s medal is for **developing** that process, together with a precise geometric theory of planar Brownian paths and a mathematically controlled interface with two-dimensional conformal field theory. The central collaboration is **Lawler–Schramm–Werner (LSW)**. Schramm died in 2008 and never received a Fields Medal; any honest seminar on Werner’s citation begins by writing Schramm’s name next to the process that now bears it.

This essay is for learners who have met Brownian motion and conformal maps and want the slogans that make 2006 intelligible: what $$\mathrm{SLE}_\kappa$$ is, why a Brownian frontier can have dimension $$4/3$$, what restriction and intersection exponents are, and how later lattice theorems of [Smirnov]({{ site.baseurl }}/contents/en/chapter02/02_28_Smirnov_Percolation/) and [Duminil-Copin]({{ site.baseurl }}/contents/en/chapter02/02_19_Duminil_Copin_Phase_Transitions/) plug discrete models into this continuum machine.

## Learning objectives

After this lecture you should be able to:

- State that **SLE** is a one-parameter family of random growing hulls driven by Brownian motion through Loewner’s equation, introduced by **Schramm** (2000).
- Explain Werner’s 2006 citation as **development** of SLE, plus 2D Brownian geometry, plus CFT links—not as sole authorship of the process.
- Describe the **LSW** theorem that the outer boundary (frontier) of planar Brownian motion has Hausdorff dimension $$4/3$$ almost surely (Mandelbrot’s conjecture).
- Define, at slogan level, **intersection exponents** and **restriction** as properties that let one compute dimensions and recognize laws.
- Name predicted $$\kappa$$ values for a few models ($$\kappa=6$$ percolation, $$\kappa=2$$ loop-erased random walk, $$\kappa=8$$ uniform spanning tree) while distinguishing **proved identifications** from **conjectures**.
- Credit Schramm explicitly, including the fact that he did not receive a Fields Medal and died in 2008.
- Cross-locate Smirnov’s lattice conformal invariance and Duminil-Copin’s phase-transition work as discrete counterparts, not as 2006 theorems.

**Prerequisites.** Complex analysis of simply connected domains (Riemann mapping, at least as a black box); Brownian motion as a continuous process in the plane; Hausdorff dimension as a notion of size between length and area. Stochastic calculus is helpful for the Loewner driving function but is not required to follow the slogans.

**Seminar links.** LO1 / LO4 (a new process that became a language; discrete 2D models ↔ continuum curves). Pair with [Smirnov]({{ site.baseurl }}/contents/en/chapter02/02_28_Smirnov_Percolation/) and [Duminil-Copin]({{ site.baseurl }}/contents/en/chapter02/02_19_Duminil_Copin_Phase_Transitions/). Same-congress neighbour: [Perelman]({{ site.baseurl }}/contents/en/chapter02/02_03_Perelman_Poincare/)—a completely different 2006 story.

## 1. Loewner’s equation, then Schramm’s noise

In 1923, Charles Loewner (then Karl Löwner) encoded a growing family of simply connected plane domains by a differential equation for their Riemann maps. If $$K_t$$ is a hull growing in the upper half-plane (or disk), the normalized conformal map $$g_t$$ from the complement onto the half-plane satisfies, in the chordal normalization,

$$
\partial_t g_t(z) = \frac{2}{g_t(z)-\xi(t)},
$$

where the real driving function $$\xi(t)$$ records how the hull attaches to the boundary. Deterministic Loewner theory is classical geometric function theory.

Schramm’s insight was to let $$\xi$$ be **Brownian motion**: $$\xi(t)=\sqrt{\kappa}\,B_t$$. The resulting random hulls are **chordal $$\mathrm{SLE}_\kappa$$**. A single parameter $$\kappa>0$$ interpolates radically different geometries: for small $$\kappa$$ the curve is a simple path; at larger $$\kappa$$ it becomes self-touching; for still larger $$\kappa$$ it is space-filling (the precise phase transitions were later established in the SLE literature). Schramm proved a classification theorem: if a random chordal curve arises as a scaling limit that is conformally invariant and satisfies a natural domain Markov property, then it must be $$\mathrm{SLE}_\kappa$$ for some $$\kappa$$. That is why SLE is not “one more random fractal.” It is the **only** candidate compatible with the symmetries 2D statistical physics had been advertising.

The 2000 paper already conjectured the dictionary: loop-erased random walk should be $$\mathrm{SLE}_2$$, critical percolation interfaces $$\mathrm{SLE}_6$$, uniform spanning tree branches $$\mathrm{SLE}_8$$. Turning those conjectures into theorems required both SLE calculus and lattice input.

## 2. What LSW actually did

Gregory Lawler, Oded Schramm, and Wendelin Werner developed the analytic and probabilistic theory of SLE far enough to compute quantities that had been inaccessible. Three families of results dominate a first portrait.

**Restriction.** Some random sets in a domain $$D$$ have the property that their law, conditioned to stay in a subdomain $$D'\subset D$$, coincides with the law defined directly in $$D'$$. Brownian excursion and certain SLE-related sets satisfy restriction; the property can be used as a recognition principle and as a computational device. LSW characterized restriction measures and related them to $$\mathrm{SLE}_{8/3}$$ and to Brownian frontiers.

**Intersection and disconnection exponents.** How likely is it that several planar Brownian paths, or several SLE hulls, avoid one another, or fail to disconnect a point from infinity? These probabilities decay as power laws; the powers are **exponents**. LSW computed the intersection exponents of planar Brownian motion and the corresponding SLE exponents, confirming predictions from physics (including Duplantier’s quantum-gravity heuristics) that had stood as conjectures.

**Brownian geometry.** Mandelbrot, comparing simulations with self-avoiding walks, conjectured that the **outer boundary** (frontier) of a planar Brownian path has Hausdorff dimension $$4/3$$. LSW proved it. The announcement “The dimension of the planar Brownian frontier is $$4/3$$,” *Math. Res. Lett.* 8 (2001), 401–411, arXiv:math/0010165, outlines a series of papers: the dimension is $$2-\xi(2,0)$$, and the relevant disconnection/intersection exponent is $$\xi(2,0)=2/3$$. The same circle of ideas gives dimension $$3/4$$ for the set of cut points. This is the “geometry of two-dimensional Brownian motion” in the IMU sentence: not the existence of Brownian motion (Wiener), but the **fractal anatomy** of its hull.

Werner’s earlier work with Lawler on Brownian intersection exponents (for example *Ann. Probab.* 27 (1999)) is part of the runway. The SLE machine made the exponents computable in closed form.

## 3. CFT as a neighbour, and lattice models: who proved what

Two-dimensional conformal field theory had predicted many of the same exponents, and Cardy’s formula, long before SLE, using operator algebras and Coulomb-gas or quantum-gravity heuristics whose mathematical status varied. SLE supplied a probability space of curves on which some predictions could be proved; conversely, SLE calculations gave geometric meaning to central charges. The IMU’s third clause is this two-way traffic, not a claim that Werner rebuilt axiomatic CFT. Slogan: **SLE made a region of CFT into theorems about random sets**.

SLE is a continuum process. A lattice model is identified with $$\mathrm{SLE}_\kappa$$ only after one proves a scaling limit and matches $$\kappa$$.

- **Loop-erased random walk and uniform spanning trees.** LSW identified them with $$\mathrm{SLE}_2$$ and $$\mathrm{SLE}_8$$ in the settings of their papers.
- **Critical percolation.** Schramm predicted $$\kappa=6$$. Smirnov (2001) proved Cardy and conformal invariance for **critical site percolation on the triangular lattice**; later work (Camia–Newman and others) identified the exploration path with $$\mathrm{SLE}_6$$. That lattice theorem is [Smirnov’s 2010 Fields]({{ site.baseurl }}/contents/en/chapter02/02_28_Smirnov_Percolation/), not the 2006 citation.
- **Ising interfaces.** Fermionic observables and later papers (Smirnov; Chelkak–Smirnov; Chelkak–Duminil-Copin–Hongler–Kemppainen–Smirnov, arXiv:1312.0533) attach $$\mathrm{SLE}_3$$ to spin interfaces in the appropriate setup.
- **Self-avoiding walk.** Predicted $$\mathrm{SLE}_{8/3}$$; still a famous conjecture.

Duminil-Copin’s later discrete phase-transition work is a counterpart, not a 2006 theorem: [Duminil-Copin]({{ site.baseurl }}/contents/en/chapter02/02_19_Duminil_Copin_Phase_Transitions/).

Schramm was born 10 December 1961. Fields Medals are age-restricted (fortieth birthday not before 1 January of the congress year); he was ineligible in 2002 by weeks, died on 1 September 2008 in a hiking accident in Washington State, and never received a Fields Medal. The 2006 SIAM Pólya Prize went jointly to Lawler, Schramm, and Werner. A fair sentence: **Schramm defined the process; LSW made it compute Brownian and restriction geometry; Smirnov and later authors fed it lattice models.** Before SLE, 2D critical phenomena had rich physics and thin continuum theorems; after LSW one could compute a Brownian frontier’s dimension and evaluate exponents by Itô calculus on Loewner maps. The 2006 class also included Okounkov, Perelman (who declined), and Tao.

## Common confusions

| Claim | Correction |
|-------|------------|
| “Werner invented SLE.” | **Schramm** invented SLE (2000). Werner developed it, largely with Lawler and Schramm. |
| “The Brownian path has dimension $$4/3$$.” | The **path** is almost surely of dimension $$2$$. The **frontier** (outer boundary of the filled hull) has dimension $$4/3$$. |
| “LSW proved conformal invariance of all 2D models.” | They built the continuum calculus and identified some models. Percolation on the triangle is Smirnov; many models remain open (e.g. self-avoiding walk). |
| “Schramm received a Fields Medal.” | He did not. He died in 2008. |
| “CFT is obsolete after SLE.” | SLE proved a region of CFT predictions; it did not delete the physics literature. |
| “$$\kappa$$ is chosen freely for each picture.” | Existence plus conformal invariance plus domain Markov **forces** some $$\kappa$$; matching a lattice model is a theorem or a conjecture. |

## Exercises

1. Write Loewner’s chordal equation and say, in one sentence, what the driving function $$\xi(t)$$ encodes.
2. Why does adding Brownian noise to $$\xi$$ produce a **classification** of conformally invariant chordal scaling limits, not just “another random curve”?
3. Distinguish carefully: Hausdorff dimension of a Brownian **path**, of its **cut points**, and of its **frontier**. Which number is $$4/3$$?
4. Restriction in your own words: a law on subsets of $$D$$ that is compatible with passing to subdomains. Why might that help compute a dimension?
5. Credit table: one line each for Schramm (2000), LSW (exponents / frontier), Smirnov (2001), Duminil-Copin (later discrete phase transitions).
6. Which of the following are theorems and which are predictions, as of this essay: LERW $$=\mathrm{SLE}_2$$; triangular site percolation interfaces $$\sim\mathrm{SLE}_6$$; SAW $$=\mathrm{SLE}_{8/3}$$?
7. **Accuracy practice.** Rewrite “Werner showed that Brownian motion is conformal” as two sentences this course would accept.
8. **Seminar stretch.** After reading [Smirnov]({{ site.baseurl }}/contents/en/chapter02/02_28_Smirnov_Percolation/), explain why Cardy’s formula and “the exploration path is $$\mathrm{SLE}_6$$” are related but not identical statements.
