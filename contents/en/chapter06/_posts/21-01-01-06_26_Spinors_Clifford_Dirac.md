Rotating a vector by $$360^\circ$$ returns it to itself. Rotating a **spinor**—the mathematical home of spin-$$1/2$$ particles—by $$360^\circ$$ multiplies it by $$-1$$; only $$720^\circ$$ restores the original. That double cover is encoded by **Spin groups**, **Clifford algebras**, and the **Dirac operator**, which Dirac introduced (1928) to make a first-order relativistic wave equation whose square recovers the Klein–Gordon operator.

This P2 map connects representation theory, geometry, and the index theorem’s favorite elliptic operator (**LO3**, **LO4**). Pair with [Lie groups]({{ site.baseurl }}/contents/en/chapter03/03_11_Lie_Groups_Particle_Physics/), [Hilbert / QM]({{ site.baseurl }}/contents/en/chapter06/06_18_Hilbert_Spectral_QM/), [Atiyah–Singer]({{ site.baseurl }}/contents/en/chapter08/08_11_Atiyah_Singer_Index/).

## 1. Spin without mysticism

The group $$SO(3)$$ of rotations has fundamental group $$\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$$. Its simply connected double cover is $$SU(2)\cong\mathrm{Spin}(3)$$. Representations of $$SU(2)$$ that do **not** factor through $$SO(3)$$ are projective representations of rotations—exactly spin half-integers.

**One-step mechanism (LO4).** Quantum amplitudes for spin-$$1/2$$ transform under $$SU(2)$$, not only under $$3\times 3$$ rotation matrices—so a $$360^\circ$$ loop can yield a minus sign (observable in interference / neutron experiments at the level of physical quantum mechanics).

## 2. Clifford algebras

Given a quadratic form (e.g. Euclidean or Minkowski metric), the **Clifford algebra** is generated by vectors with relation

$$
vw + wv = 2\langle v,w\rangle.
$$

In physics notation, Dirac matrices satisfy

$$
\{\gamma^\mu,\gamma^\nu\} = 2\eta^{\mu\nu}.
$$

Spinor spaces are modules over Clifford algebras; the Dirac operator is built by contracting Clifford multiplication with differentiation (and connections when coupled to geometry or gauge fields).

---

## 3. The Dirac equation (map)

Free Dirac equation in Minkowski space:

$$
(i\gamma^\mu \partial_\mu - m)\psi = 0.
$$

Squaring (with care) yields a Klein–Gordon-type second-order equation for each component—relativistic dispersion—while keeping a first-order Hamiltonian structure suitable for quantum interpretation. Historical payoff included spin-$$1/2$$ and the prediction of antimatter (positron), later observed.

Coupling to electromagnetism replaces $$\partial_\mu$$ by $$D_\mu=\partial_\mu+iqA_\mu$$—gauge covariance ([gauge geometry]({{ site.baseurl }}/contents/en/chapter06/06_19_Gauge_Geometry_YM/)).

## 4. Geometry and index theory
