Some mathematics is ancient and finished-looking. Some is ancient and unfinished. And some is so new that its textbooks are still being written in real time.

This chapter lives in that third zone: the **frontiers** where mathematics is being built because the world—or the internal logic of the subject—demands it. Artificial intelligence forces fresh questions about high-dimensional geometry, optimization, and generalization. Quantum information rewrites what “information” can mean. Networks, biology, cryptography after quantum computers, optimal transport, complexity, mathematical physics, and the **principle of duality** (math ↔ physics dictionaries) keep inventing objects that do not sit politely inside nineteenth-century syllabi.

The tone here is deliberately unfinished. You will meet definitions that are stable and claims that are still conjectural; tools that already run the internet and theories that may underwrite the next decade of models. The skill to practice is not prophecy. It is **literacy under uncertainty**: what is theorem, what is heuristic, what is hype.

If Chapter 1 is “questions that would not die,” this chapter is “questions that have only just learned to speak.”
