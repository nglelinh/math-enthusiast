Research does not begin with a solution. It begins with a **question you can live with for a while**—and a notebook.

This chapter is a studio, not a lecture hall. The prompts are deliberately open: How small can a Kakeya set be? How efficiently can spheres pack? How predictable are primes? Can randomness create order? What would a fourth dimension *feel* like in coordinates? How many colors does a map need? Can mathematics describe infinity? What happens when a simple rule is iterated forever?

There may be no tidy answer waiting at the end of the week. That is not a defect. That is the point.

Here you practice the habits that actual mathematics requires after the textbook ends: form a conjecture, design a small experiment, draw a picture, write down a failure, revise the question, separate observation from proof. The Collatz studio is the seminar’s default deep exploration; the other prompts are alternate doors into the same craft.

Bring curiosity. Bring honesty. Leave with a log that would make a future self proud—even if the theorem remains open.
