## Knowledge units (reconstructed)

### Kakeya needle problem (0–55s)

Unit needle: reverse through all directions with minimal area. Pivot disk has area π/4≈0.785; smarter continuous motions can do better.

### Besicovitch zero-area sets via Perron trees (100–160s)

Besicovitch: Kakeya *sets* (static unit segment every direction) can have Lebesgue measure zero via Perron-tree cutting-and-sliding. Continuous needle motion can have arbitrarily small positive area but not measure zero.

### Hausdorff dimension and Kakeya conjecture (160–230s)

Zero area ≠ thin: measure with Hausdorff/box dimension. Conjecture: every Kakeya set in R^n has dim_H = dim_M = n. Plane: Davies 1971 dim=2.

### Fefferman and harmonic analysis (200–250s)

Around 1971 Fefferman links Kakeya geometry to central Fourier questions; Kakeya becomes foundation of an analysis tower.

### 3D lower bounds to Wang–Zahl (250–340s)

### Tower above Kakeya (340–436s)
