## Knowledge units (reconstructed)

### Fourier restriction as curvature vs energy focus (50–150s)

Restriction asks how much a Fourier transform of a measure supported on a curved hypersurface (sphere) can concentrate. Flat directions allow coherent focusing along lines; curvature forces cancellation. Knapp example: small spherical cap ≈ flat ⇒ energy lives in a thin tube.

### Wave packets = tubes; Restriction ⇒ Kakeya (150–240s)

Partition sphere into caps → each packet lives in a long thin tube with radius ~ sqrt(length) scaling relative to cap size; all directions appear. Tube-overlap control is needed for restriction, so Kakeya-type geometry is necessary (not sufficient).

### Progress ledger toward p=3 (240–380s)

Stein–Tomas 1975: p≥4. Then Bourgain, Tao, Bourgain–Guth, Guth polynomial partitioning, broom structures, Wang collaborators: progressive improvements; late-2024 popular account cites 22/7≈3.143 with remaining gap ≈0.143 to the believed critical 3. Exponents across papers are not always identical formulations—compare carefully.

### Two-ends geometric reduction (Wang–Wu ~2024) (380–500s)

Instead of pure analytic inequalities, reduce restriction to a geometric intersection hypothesis for tube families with a two-ends mass condition (mass cannot pile only at one end). If two-ends holds in 3D, restriction would follow. Plane analogues partly settled; 3D open. Links to Furstenberg technology.

### Post-Fields open race (500–580s)

After Kakeya foundation (2025) and Wang Fields 2026, the video frames restriction + related towers as the next decade's prize landscape—not a prediction of winners.
