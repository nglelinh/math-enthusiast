## 2. Key definitions

### Countable

In bijection with a subset of $$\mathbb{N}$$ (or finite).

### Diagonal construction

Given listed sequences $$a_i=(a_{i1},a_{i2},\ldots)$$, define $$b_n \neq a_{nn}$$ so $$b$$ is not any $$a_i$$.


---

## 3. Formal status / theorem (seminar honesty)

**Status:** **Proved** (Cantor). Uncountability of $$\mathbb{R}$$ / power set of $$\mathbb{N}$$ is standard theorem; representation hygiene is the main student pitfall.

**Statement / slogan:**

No surjection $$\mathbb{N}\to\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}$$ (equivalently $$\mathbb{R}$$ is uncountable). Diagonal flips $$a_{nn}$$ to build an escaped sequence.
